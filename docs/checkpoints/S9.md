## 1. What S9 inherited

S10 pinned the `(content × candidate-style × reference-style)` matrix and reported its
uncomfortable headline: **a style's own reference is the best match in only 3/27 rows**,
with the candidate rendered at 200 strokes against 700-stroke references (S10 §3.1, the
stated cost confound). S9's brief: (a) remove the cost confound, (b) find the
weighting/heuristics under which the diagonal wins, H5-gated.

## 2. The instrument — why it needs no browser

The composite is an **exact linear function of five distance terms**
(`STYLE_MATCH_WEIGHTS`, `js/engine/metrics.js`):

```
styleMatch = 1 − Σₖ wₖ · partₖ        w = {scale .30, orientation .25, coverage .20, bandScale .15, coherence .10}
```

Every pinned cell carries its five `parts`, so a row's margin decomposes exactly into
five **weighted contributions** `Σₖ wₖ·(ownPartₖ − otherPartₖ)` (positive ⇒ own is WORSE
on that term), and the whole matrix can be **re-ranked offline under any weights**. So
both S9 questions are answerable from the pinned artefact with no re-render —
`style-accuracy-sweep.cjs` does exactly that.

## 3. The correlation report — which term drives the loss

Mean weighted contribution per term, over the 27 rows (positive = hurts the diagonal):

| term | ALL | WIN rows | LOSS rows | range over ALL rows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| scale (.30) | **+0.0294** | −0.0595 | +0.0405 | [−0.1612, +0.1983] |
| orientation (.25) | **+0.0571** | +0.0003 | +0.0642 | [**−0.0020**, +0.2436] |
| coverage (.20) | −0.0010 | −0.0244 | +0.0019 | [−0.0909, +0.0757] |
| bandScale (.15) | −0.0000 | −0.0099 | +0.0012 | [−0.0352, +0.0305] |
| coherence (.10) | −0.0020 | −0.0033 | −0.0018 | [−0.0323, +0.0170] |

**The finding.** `orientation` is the dominant driver of the loss and it is **almost
perfectly one-sided against the candidate**: across all 27 rows its contribution never
helps own by more than −0.0020, yet it hurts own by up to **+0.2436**. `scale` is the
second, also net-positive (+0.0294). Coverage / bandScale / coherence are
near-neutral in aggregate. In other words the candidate's orientation (and, less so,
scale) signature is offset from **every** reference's in the same direction — the mark
of a render-path/content bias, not of "the wrong style being picked".

**Who steals the diagonal:** `watercolor` (9 rows) and `marker` (8) are the most common
runners-up; `charcoal` is the only style that wins consistently (2 of the 3 wins).

## 4. The weight sweep — re-weighting REJECTED

`style-accuracy-sweep.cjs` sweeps the weight simplex (step 0.05 → 10 626 weightings) and
re-ranks the full matrix under each. Result on the pinned matrix:

```
BASELINE   ownIsBest  3/27   meanMargin −0.0834
BEST       ownIsBest  9/27   meanMargin −0.0332  (scale .05 orientation .05 coverage .15 bandScale .30 coherence .45)
```

**Re-weighting nearly triples the diagonal (3 → 9/27) but the mean margin stays
NEGATIVE and two-thirds of the rows are still losses.** A weighting is therefore *not*
the fix, and shipping one would have been a 20 % headline bought by re-tuning five
constants against the very matrix it is measured on — precisely what the H5 gate exists
to refuse. Rejected, deliberately.

## 5. The cost confound — measured, not the driver

`--strokes=200 --ref-strokes=200` re-runs the whole matrix **cost-matched** (references
and candidate at the same 200 strokes):

```
cost-matched (200/200)   ownIsBest  5/27   meanMargin −0.1199
  + best re-weighting    ownIsBest  9/27   meanMargin −0.0730
```

Cost-matching moves the diagonal by **+2 rows** (3 → 5/27); the ceiling under any
weighting is still **9/27**. So the 200-vs-700 gap is real but minor: removing it, and
re-weighting on top, leaves the majority of rows lost.

## 6. Verdict

**The diagonal loss is structural.** Neither the cost gap (5/27) nor the best possible
weighting (9/27), nor both together, makes a style's own reference win more than a third
of the time. The same ~9/27 ceiling appearing under two different cost regimes and
different weight shapes points at the **feature definition**, not its blend: the S1
`RefPacket` terms do not yet carry enough style-discriminating signal to separate
near-neighbours (the same limitation H-S5 recorded — adjacent references 96.4 % identical
in orientation). The honest next step is a **feature-space** change (e.g. render the
references through the *same* style-aware backend as the candidate, so the only
difference left is the style; or add a discriminating term), measured the same way, and
H5-gated. **S9 does not claim a fix; it hands one a target and a ruler.**
